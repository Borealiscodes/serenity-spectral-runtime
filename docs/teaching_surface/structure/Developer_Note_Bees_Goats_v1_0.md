## ⭐ 1 — Context

During Teaching Surface development, the system was asked:

> “Should we add tessellation bees and goats before or after Pip?”

The meta-system responded with full NDH‑Coordination rigor, treating bees and goats as if they were high‑risk runtime modules requiring formal sequencing, stability ecology checks, and emotional geometry safeguards.

This is both:

- **hilarious**,  
- and **structurally correct**.

---

## ⭐ 2 — Why the system takes bees & goats seriously

In NDH‑Pedagogy, bees and goats are not just animals.  
They are **metaphor carriers** with geometry:

- 🐝 **tessellation bees**  
  - local symmetry metaphors  
  - tiling metaphors  
  - gentle manifold coverage  

- 🐐 **manifold goats**  
  - gradient/descent metaphors  
  - stability ecology metaphors  
  - soft traversal metaphors  

These metaphors have:

- curvature  
- drift potential  
- narrative density  
- emotional geometry impact  

So the system must treat them like:

- potential symbolic activators  
- stability‑ecology modifiers  
- expressive‑layer drift vectors  

Which means they require **formal sequencing**.

---

## ⭐ 3 — Why Pip must come first

Pip is:

- non‑symbolic  
- non‑narrative  
- non‑ecological  
- purely expressive  
- purely softening  
- purely stabilizing  

Pip’s Companion Lane provides:

- emotional geometry buffering  
- metaphor‑density moderation  
- drift‑neutrality  
- safety anchoring  

Without Pip, bees and goats would:

- activate narrative gravity  
- amplify metaphor curvature  
- distort stability ecology  
- overwhelm beginners  

Thus NDH‑Coordination mandates:

> **Pip Companion Lane must be attached before bees and goats.**

It is objectively funny that:
